The strategy in five lines

  1. Claim everywhere now, fill city by city. Spreading wide works for events and business directories (Eventbrite, Yelp). It fails for trade between private people: Craigslist is barely used in Germany, Kijiji failed in France, and Homejoy opened 30 cities in 6 months and shut down. So every country gets events, businesses, jobs and services early, and rooms, items and cars open per city.
  2. Race for the big diasporas first. Canada (≈281k), the US (≈750k) and Australia (≈96k) start before Nowruz. Nowruz events are promoted 1–3 months ahead, so starting in March would miss them.
  3. Density beats features. Features are easy to copy. Density in a city, founding businesses with their history and reviews, partner Telegram channels and an early lead in search results are hard to copy.
  4. Every country stands alone. Separate health checks, a moderator for each time zone, and a fallback for the server, payments and phone checks.
  5. Money from businesses, never from risk. Pro and verification are sold only to businesses outside the EU. Turkey is paid business-to-business only. Nothing touches Iran.
Competitors already exist. Irania (Trex Innovations, app version 1.0 on 25 Aug 2026) says it covers 57 cities in 26 countries, including Toronto, Vancouver, LA, Sydney and Melbourne. It sells business sponsorships at $19.99–99.99 and matches people who carry parcels to Iran, which Peydoo bans. IraniLink (Manchester) says it serves the UK and Turkey, with 689 businesses and 209 classifieds. Both figures come from their own pages. Neither has density yet, and that is the window.

Waitlist data: unconfirmed sign-ups are deleted after 30 days. Lists for countries with no plan are deleted after 12 months, with a notice first.

No single point of failure

Round 6 had one shared brake: if Berlin was slow, nothing else would start. You were right that this is a weak point. It is replaced by three rules:

Each country on its own

Each country has its own health check. A country that misses stays in a quiet Starter: still live, never closed, with less promotion and an honest message. If Berlin is slow, only Germany's next cities wait.

A capacity check for new openings

Before anything new opens, all of these must be green:

  • listings reviewed within 12 hours in every region for 4 weeks;
  • moderator hours covered;
  • server memory under 70% at peak;
  • a restore tested in the last 90 days;
  • your own week under its cap.

If one is red, only new openings wait. Countries already live carry on.

Turkey makes money

Screening isn't what costs money. The EU, OFAC and UN sanctions lists are free (the UK's OFSI list closed in January 2026). A daily check of new list entries against our few hundred names costs nothing to run and takes 2–4 days to build. A paid check at sign-up costs about €0.40 per business. The real costs are your 20–40 minutes per business, the Turkish data-protection representative and the legal opinions. The fees are set to cover them.

  • Who can pay: only companies with a tax number (VKN) and a MERSİS number. Selling to businesses avoids registering for Turkish VAT, because the buyer self-assesses it (sorumlu sıfatıyla). Sole traders are listed, but pay by bank transfer until Stripe answers. Associations, lawyers and clinics get free, neutral listings, because Turkish rules restrict their advertising.
  • Order: free application, then the sanctions check before any money, then payment, then manual verification, then a daily re-check. A yearly re-verification is paid by the renewal. Money from a listed person can't be refunded: it has to be frozen and reported to the Bundesbank.
  • Tax: the Turkish payer withholds 15% (Decision 476), so plan on receiving 85%. Business sales to Turkey aren't taxable in Germany, so they probably don't count toward the §19 limits (for the tax advisor).
  • Payment: Stripe only after it confirms in writing (next section). Otherwise yearly invoices paid by bank transfer in euros. Paddle forbids advertising services. iyzico and PayTR need a Turkish company.
  • Year 1, Istanbul (my estimate, about 50 businesses at most because of your time): about €1.8k, €4.0k or €7.4k gross (low, middle, high). That covers screening, card fees and the representative (€1–3k a year), but not the one-off legal opinions (€2–6k). Break-even is realistic in year 2. Turkey's real value in year 1 is being there first.
  • Start: Istanbul in June 2027, paid from day one, after four preconditions:
    1. the sanctions lawyer;
    2. Stripe's written answer;
    3. the tax and Turkish advisors;
    4. VERBIS registration and the data-protection representative.

The biggest risk: Stripe's "citizen of Iran" clause

This concerns all of Peydoo, not just Turkey. Stripe's restricted list (checked 22 Sep 2026) bans dealings with persons "located in, resident in, or a citizen of" Iran. Many Iranians in Germany and elsewhere hold only an Iranian passport, and many of the businesses that would buy Pro are owned by Iranian citizens. Read literally, the clause covers part of our core market. Founding Pro is free for 6 months, so the first payments come around May 2027. Before then:
  • send Stripe a written question (I'll draft it): may an EU business sell advertising to Iranian citizens living in the EU, Canada and so on, and to companies they own?
  • set up Mollie as a second provider and check its terms;
  • have the sanctions lawyer check the setup.
