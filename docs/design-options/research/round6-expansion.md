# Research 6: every country within 12 months?

Peydoo · 2026-10-25 · web research plus repo docs; no code changes. This builds on `docs/strategy.md` (deep, not wide; gates), `docs/design-options/research/round5-countries.md` (country facts and the old order) and `round5-dark-waitlist-pro.md` (waitlist gate, pioneer rewards, pre-listings). It does not repeat their figures except where needed.

**Legal and tax points in this report are background, not legal or tax advice.** **[1 source]** marks a claim I could confirm in only one source. "(estimate)" marks my own numbers.

**The answer in one paragraph:** opening **every target country within 12 months is realistic if "live" means different things in different countries**. All twelve can be live by October 2027: three as **Guide** or **Online** pages, all the rest at least in **Starter** mode (events, verified businesses, online services, jobs, with a waitlist for person-to-person categories per city). **Opening person-to-person housing, items and cars in every country within 12 months is not realistic for one person**, and the evidence says trying would hurt Berlin. A realistic target is **5–8 "Open" cities by October 2027**: Berlin, Hamburg, Vienna, the Randstad, Toronto, London (rooms first), Milan (rooms for the September intake), and Los Angeles if a moderator in the Americas is in place. This changes three things in `docs/strategy.md`: the UK, Turkey and the UAE are no longer "closed in year one"; the US moves from year 3 to month 5 (Starter); and the "next country" gate becomes a "next Open city" gate, because Starter needs no gate.

## 1. Evidence: wide or deep launches

### What happened elsewhere

| Platform | What they did | What happened | Lesson for Peydoo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OLX** (2006–) | Went into many countries quickly. It started in India, not in its home country Argentina, where it waited 4.5 years. Heavy TV spending ([Fortune 2014](https://fortune.com/2014/10/29/olx-emerging-markets)). More than 90 countries and 50 languages under Grinda ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabrice_Grinda)). Avoided the US, Germany, the UK and France ([Emerce 2010](https://www.emerce.nl/?p=19096)) [1 source]. | Being present everywhere did not make it a leader everywhere. Naspers later **bought or merged local leaders** and rebranded them as OLX: 11 countries in 2014 alone ([BusinessTech](https://businesstech.co.za/news/?p=68614)). Many markets were later sold or closed, for example Avito/Russia in 2022 and the Autos business in India, Indonesia and Turkey ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/news/olx-layoffs-hit-800-employees-as-group-shuts-down-some-markets-report-news-296627)). | A wide footprint is cheap. Winning a country took concentrated money or a local leader. |
| **Kijiji / eBay Classifieds** | Launched in **6 countries on the same day** (Feb 2005: Canada, China, France, Germany, Italy, Japan), in more than 50 cities ([Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/technology/ebay-launches-local-on-line-classified-ad-service/article20420224)). | **Canada became the leader**, where Craigslist was weak ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kijiji)). **France failed against leboncoin** and closed ([FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/la-chute-daudience-de-kijiji-fr-lenvoit-au-terminus/3996)). In Germany it worked only after the 2009 merger into **eBay Kleinanzeigen**, which borrowed eBay.de's traffic: about 8M visitors a month at the start, about 30M by 2018 ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.de/nachrichten/einzelhandel/marktfuehrer-zehn-jahre-ebay-kleinanzeigen/2019090632984)). | A wide launch is a lottery. You win where there is no incumbent or where you can borrow traffic. For Peydoo the borrowed traffic is the Telegram partner channels. |
| **Craigslist** | One city at a time: SF, then 8 US cities in 2000; the first foreign city (Vancouver) in 2001; a large international batch in 2004 ([official list](https://www.craigslist.org/about/expansion), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craigslist)). About 700 cities by 2023. | In Germany it is "technically there" but "largely unused" ([The Local, 2026](https://www.thelocal.de/20260602/whats-the-german-version-of-craigslist)) [1 source]. | **Having a city page is not the same as having liquidity.** Empty city pages last for decades. |
| **Gumtree** | London in 2000, for a **diaspora** (Australians, New Zealanders, South Africans in London). **31 cities by 2005**, including Warsaw and Berlin, and profitable when eBay bought it ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gumtree)). | It won the UK, Australia and South Africa: the places the founding diaspora came from and moved between. Continental cities stayed minor. | A diaspora carries a platform to the places it moves between. This is Peydoo's best case: the same people, the same language, and Telegram links between cities. |
| **Facebook Marketplace** | Oct 2016: 4 English-speaking countries, built on **450M people a month already using buy-and-sell groups** ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2016/10/introducing-marketplace-buy-and-sell-with-your-local-community/)). Then Canada, Mexico and Chile, and **17 European countries in Aug 2017** ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/facebook-brings-marketplace-17-european-countries), [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2017/08/15/facebook-marketplace-countries-europe/)). | It worked fast because the liquidity already existed. **It staged categories**: vehicles (Oct 2017) and rentals (Nov 2018) launched **US-only** with partner feeds ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2017/10/26/facebook-marketplace-used-car-dealers/), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/facebook-meta/2017/facebook-marketplace-lists-apartment-rentals/)). | **Wide is fine when the network already exists. Categories that need density can open per country later.** |
| **Vinted** | Moved one country at a time and **connected each new country to an existing catalogue**. Italy was market #13 in 2020 and the Netherlands came through buying United Wardrobe in Oct 2020 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinted)) [1 source]; Portugal in 2021 ([Team France Export](https://www.teamfrance-export.fr/infos-sectorielles/7538/7538-la-startup-lituanienne-vinted-arrive-au-portugal)); Ireland in Nov 2024, linked to the French catalogue ([TheJournal](https://www.thejournal.ie/article.php?id=6539263), [University Times](https://universitytimes.ie/2025/09/vinteds-position-in-ireland-for-college-students/)); and opened DE–NL trading in 2024 ([FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.de/nachrichten/einzelhandel/vinted-offnet-internationale-handelsmoglichkeiten-fur-deutsche-mitglieder/2024101058522)). | A new country **never opens empty**, because it sees the existing catalogue on day 1. | Peydoo's equivalent is the **cross-border online layer**: online services, online events and remote jobs are visible in every country from day 1. |
| **Etsy** | Effectively global from early on. **One third of transactions crossed borders** by 2013, followed by machine translation ([EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2014/01/31/etsy-ceo-chad-dickerson-reflects-2013/)). | In 2015–16 it **started ranking local sellers higher** in the UK, Australia, France and Germany, because "buyers often prefer local" ([EcommerceBytes](https://www.ecommercebytes.com/2016/04/14/etsy-makes-changes-search/)). | Even goods that ship worldwide end up with a local preference. Show local first and the online layer second. |
| **Airbnb** | Global demand from day 1, because travel crosses borders by nature. Bought the Hamburg clone Accoleo in 2011 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/06/01/airbnb-buys-german-clone-accoleo-opens-first-european-office-in-hamburg//%7D%7D)) and had **9 international offices by spring 2012** (Blitzscaling excerpt, [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/9517341-in-the-end-airbnb-s-founders-realized-that-they-wanted-to)) [1 source]. | Local teams came **after** the global product, to fight clones on supply. | Cross-border categories can go global from the start. Local effort follows where usage appears. |
| **Eventbrite** | A self-serve tool for organisers everywhere. Offices came later: London, Dublin, Melbourne. Melbourne was chosen because the **customer base was already there**, split evenly between Sydney and Melbourne ([SmartCompany](https://stagecdn.smartcompany.com.au/?p=42163)) [1 source]. 27–30% of revenue was non-US by 2016–17 ([S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001475115/000119312518255960/d593770ds1.htm)). | Events need only one side, the organiser, to have value. | **Events can be open in every country from day 1.** |
| **Meetup** | Global from launch in June 2002, organiser-driven; 56k users within months ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meetup)). | Worked because every group creates its own small network. | Same as events. |
| **Thumbtack** | Launched in Dec 2009 with **every service category in every US region**, which went against standard marketplace advice. It found suppliers by crawling and emailing them in bulk, and gave them a single-player tool for posting to Craigslist ([Sharetribe](https://www.sharetribe.com/academy/why-thumbtack-succeeded/), [Vator](https://vator.tv/news/2018-09-13-when-thumbtack-was-young-the-early-years/)). | Worked for services because each pro listing is useful on its own. The named risk was customers who "come once, don't get a bid, and never come back" (Lacy, in Sharetribe). | Services and a business directory tolerate a wide launch. Requests that wait for replies do not. |
| **Yelp** | Pages could cover any city, but **a community manager was hired only once a city had "enough active users"** ([VentureBeat 2008](https://venturebeat.com/2008/02/27/yelp-raises-15-million-sees-uptick-in-growth-over-past-year)). It went from 12 to 24 cities in 2007–08, the UK in 2009 and France in 2010 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelp)). | Light presence everywhere, human effort only where traction appeared. | This is exactly the Starter → Open model. |
| **Nextdoor** | A neighbourhood went live only with 10 verified households in 21 days (round 5). Netherlands in Feb 2016 (first non-English country), then the UK in Sep 2016 after a beta with 250 neighbourhoods ([Nextdoor NL](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/corporate-announcements/nextdoor-expands-internationally-starting-with-the-netherlands), [UK](https://about.nextdoor.com/press-releases/corporate-announcements/nextdoor-launches-in-neighbourhoods-across-the-uk)). | Gates prevented dead pages, even in new countries. | Keep the gate per city, not per country. |
| **Homejoy** (failure) | About **30 cities in 6 months** after a $38M round. | Only 15–20% of customers rebooked within a month (Handy: 35%). Shut down in 2015. The causes are debated: worker-classification lawsuits as well as retention ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2015/07/31/why-homejoy-failed-and-the-future-of-the-on-demand-economy), [Scaled and Failed](https://scaledandfailed.substack.com/p/issue-24-home-services)). | Opening cities faster than you can serve them **multiplies a retention problem rather than solving it**. |

### Diaspora and community platforms across several countries

- **Gumtree** (above) is the clearest case of a diaspora platform spreading along migration routes.
- **Dubizzle** (UAE, 2005) later expanded to about 8 MENA countries, Egypt among them (exact year not confirmed) ([Amwal Alghad](https://en.amwalalghad.com/uaes-dubizzle-begins-its-work-in-egypt/)). In 2015 those countries were **rebranded as OLX**, and only the UAE kept the Dubizzle brand ([TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2015/09/02/dubizzle-will-now-be-known-as-olx-in-north-africa-and-the-middle-east/), [Wamda](https://www.wamda.com/2015/09/dubai-classifieds-platform-dubizzle-acquired-by-olx)). The home market carried the brand; the wide expansion did not.
- **Sulekha** (Indian classifieds and yellow pages) opened Indian cities **chosen by the traffic already coming from them**: 20 cities in 2008, with 15 more planned ([exchange4media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/sulekha.com-launches-in-20-tier-ii-iii-citiesplans-to-launch-in-another-15-cities-32293.html), [Campaign India](https://www.campaignindia.in/article/sulekha-com-launches-city-specific-search/408951)). I found no source on how it rolled out in the US.
- **Persian directories:**
  - LA's **"Iranian Yellow Pages / The Iranian Directory"** by Ketab Corp. was the first published outside Iran and still had about 2,500 listings in 2017 ([Jewish Journal](https://jewishjournal.com/culture/224714/224714/), [USPTO via Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/ketab-corporation-269578)).
  - A 2021 piece says that in Westwood "the directory of Iranian businesses became obsolete" and the bookshops and music stores disappeared ([Tehran Bureau](https://tehranbureau.com/the-last-days-of-westwood/)) [1 source].
  - Canada has its own online **Iranian Yellow Pages of Canada** ([Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/canada/ii-iranian-community-in-canada/)), which I did not check is still active.
  - Persian directories in North America have always been **one per country or city**, and print-era. No one built a multi-country Persian product.
- **IraniLink** is now a **mobile app covering the UK and Turkey** together: directory, classifieds, events, guides and exchange rates; released 23 Jul 2026 according to an app-store mirror ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/iranilink/6777489362), now 410) [1 source]. **A competitor is going multi-country in 2026.** That is an argument for claiming the UK in Starter mode in year 1, rather than year 2.
- **Not found or not verified:** Canadajoo, 30Ti, Javanan as a directory, Polish diaspora classifieds in the UK, Russian-speaking diaspora classifieds. My searches returned nothing usable. A Chinese-language classifieds site run by Vancouver's Buy&Sell existed in the 2000s ([Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/technology/buysell-revamps-sites/article25710883)) [1 source]. Ethnic-language classifieds are an established niche, but I have no data on how they covered several countries.

### What this means

1. **Wide works for categories where one side alone has value**: events (Eventbrite, Meetup), services and directories (Thumbtack, Yelp), and cross-border goods and stays (Etsy, Airbnb, Vinted's linked catalogues).
2. **Wide fails for person-to-person local categories without borrowed liquidity**: Craigslist in Germany, Kijiji in France, Homejoy. The "ghost town" or anti-network effect ([Chen](https://productandrew.substack.com/p/product-31)) appears when a visitor finds nothing nearby and never returns.
3. **Category staging per country is normal**: Facebook kept vehicles and rentals in the US first; Yelp added city managers only after traction.
4. **Diaspora platforms spread along the community's own links** (Gumtree). Peydoo's link is Telegram plus a single language. That makes a wide *Starter* footprint cheaper for Peydoo than for any general marketplace: one language, one moderation team, one brand.

## 2. Which categories need local density, and the three modes

### Density by category

| Category | Needs density in the city? | Why | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rooms and housing (P2P) | **Yes, high** | You need many offers in one city, and the deposit-scam risk needs fast local moderation | Open only |
| Items (P2P) | **Yes** | Pick-up is local, and a buyer needs dozens of items to browse | Open only |
| Cars | **Yes**, and legally sensitive (TR, UAE) | Local, high value, scam-prone | Open only (never TR/UAE) |
| Local jobs from named employers | Medium | One good job post is useful on its own; volume is low | Starter |
| Remote and online jobs | No | Cross-border | Online layer (everywhere) |
| Events (organiser-verified) | No | One event is useful on its own; organisers bring their own audience | Starter, and online events go everywhere |
| Business directory (verified, Pro) | Low | Each page is useful on its own (Yelp, Yellow Pages) | Starter |
| Online services (lessons, translation, consulting, therapy, immigration law) | No | Delivered at a distance | Online layer |
| Community posts and Q&A | Medium | Need some activity to feel alive | Starter, read-mostly, moderated |

### The three modes (what "live" means)

| Mode | What is visible | What can be posted | Payments | Moderation load | Countries |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Open** (per city) | Everything | All categories | Pro, bumps, featured | High (P2P scams) | City passes the Open gate (§6) |
| **Starter** (per country, with city pages) | Events, verified businesses, online services, jobs, guides; a **per-city waitlist with pre-listing drafts** for rooms, items and cars; the online layer | Events (organisers verified by hand), businesses (verified), services, employer jobs. P2P posts are **drafts only**, published on the city's opening day (round 5 pre-listings) | **Pro to businesses only.** No B2C bumps outside the EU until tax is settled (§5) | Low: verified posters only | All other target countries |
| **Guide** | Static Persian guides (official links, scam warnings), the online layer (online events and services from open countries), a waitlist where appropriate | Nothing tied to the location | None | Very low | Turkey; UAE as "Guide-lite" |
| **Online layer** (across all modes) | Online services, online events and remote jobs marked «آنلاین», shown in every country below local content | Posters from Open or Starter countries | Pro | Shared | Everywhere from Yalda |

**Product changes this needs:**
- A **country × city × category availability matrix** set in admin, not in code;
- an «آنلاین» flag on services, events and jobs;
- event time zones (show the viewer's local time);
- currency per country for Pro prices;
- a per-city, per-category waitlist with pre-listing drafts (round 5);
- honest empty states such as «اتاق در لس‌آنجلس هنوز باز نشده — در فهرست انتظار ثبت‌نام کنید (۲۱۴ نفر)».

The seed already has 37 countries and 81 cities (`docs/setup.md`), so the data model is mostly there.

**Evidence that category-staged launches work:**
- Facebook Marketplace: vehicles and rentals US-only first.
- Yelp: pages anywhere, community managers after traction.
- Thumbtack: services nationwide.
- Eventbrite: self-serve everywhere, offices after usage.
- Nextdoor: per-neighbourhood gate.

I found **no published study comparing staged and unstaged launches**. The evidence is a set of cases, so treat this as a strong pattern, not a measured effect.

**Starter has its own ghost-town risk.** A Starter city page with 2 businesses and 0 events looks dead too. Rule: a **Starter city gets its own page and promotion only with ≥10 verified businesses and ≥4 upcoming events** (proposal). Until then it appears only as a line on the country page plus the waitlist.

## 3. The United States

### Demand

- **Size:** 750k Iranian Americans in 2024 ([Pew, Mar 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/03/05/7-facts-about-iranian-americans/)).
  - By metro: **LA 230k**, NY 45k, SF 40k, Washington 40k, San Jose 30k, San Diego 25k.
  - By state: California 375k, Texas 55k (Houston and Dallas), NY 40k, Virginia 30k.
- **Profile:** older and settled.
  - Immigrants' median age is **54.3**, and 79% of immigrants are naturalised.
  - The US-born have a median age of **18.7**, a second generation that may prefer English (Pew).
  - 65% hold a bachelor's degree. This is a market of established businesses, professionals and events, not newcomers.
- **Newcomers:**
  - Close to zero since the 2026 visa suspension (round 5).
  - But **12,656 Iranian students** were already in the US in 2024/25, 82% of them graduate students ([Open Doors fact sheet](https://opendoorsdata.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/OD_Iran_Fact-Sheet-2025.pdf), [Iran Times](https://iran-times.com/iran-student-numbers-in-us-rising-again)).
  - They need rooms, cars and jobs near UCLA, USC, Berkeley, Stanford, the University of Maryland, UT Austin and others. This is a real but scattered P2P demand.
- **Events:**
  - Large and seasonal: Farhang Foundation's Nowruz concerts (Segerstrom, sold out in 2023–24; [Voice of OC](https://voiceofoc.org/?p=854848)), Mehregan at Great Park Live in Orange County ([listing](https://www.eventbrite.co.uk/d/ca--irvine/persian/)), the NYC Persian Day Parade (19 Apr 2026; [West Side Spirit](https://www.westsidespirit.com/news/persian-day-parade-brings-iranian-pride-to-madison-avenue-IA5772875)), and Tirgan's 2026 "world tour" stop in Orange County, 6–9 Aug ([tirgan.ca](https://tirgan.ca/)) [1 source].
  - **Caveat: in 2026, events are muted by the war.** Farhang **cancelled its 2026 Nowruz festival at UCLA** "in light of the ongoing and horrific loss of innocent lives in Iran" and held solemn concerts instead ([Farhang](https://farhang.org/2026/farhang-cancels-2026-nowruz-festival-at-ucla)). The 2026 parade was dedicated to victims (PIX11). Launch copy for Nowruz 2027 must be sober, not "party".
- **Businesses:** Westwood ("Tehrangeles", Persian Square) still has restaurants and services, but its retail is declining ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrangeles), [NPR 2019](https://www.npr.org/2019/07/05/736020514/iranian-american-small-businesses-struggle-under-rising-tensions)). Doctors, dentists, lawyers, realtors, insurance and mortgage brokers, and CPAs serving Persian speakers are spread across LA, Orange County, the Bay Area and DC. **This is the best Pro market Peydoo will have.** Settled professionals pay for visibility, and the printed directory is "obsolete" (Tehran Bureau).
- **Competition:** Instagram, Facebook groups, Telegram (US usage unmeasured; probably lower than in Europe for the older generation, which is my inference), Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace for P2P, Yelp and Google for businesses, and the remaining print directories. **No modern multi-city Persian directory with events was found.**

### Legal cost of serving the US from Germany (not legal advice)

| Topic | What applies | Cost or action |
|---|---|---|
| **Section 230** | Protects platforms from liability for users' content (not for federal criminal law or IP) | Free; keep "we host, users post" |

Texas TDPSA has no revenue threshold but exempts SBA-defined small businesses, **except that selling sensitive data still needs consent** ([FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/the-right-to-be-let-a-lone-star-state-texas-passes-comprehensive-privacy-bill/)) | Don't sell data (we don't). The GDPR-level privacy policy already covers most of it; add a US section |
| **Sales tax** | Economic nexus is mostly **$100k** in sales into a state; CA, TX and NY are **$500k** (NY also needs 100 transactions); 16+ states have dropped the 200-transaction test ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/states-eliminating-economic-nexus-transaction-thresholds.html), [TaxCloud 2026](https://taxcloud.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026_Sales_Tax_Nexus_Thresholds_TaxCloud.pdf), [Numeral](https://www.numeral.com/blog/economic-nexus)). | **FTC** | Consumer Review Rule (from 21 Oct 2024): no fake or bought reviews, no review suppression; **up to $53,088 per violation** in 2025 ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/five-stars-zero-tolerance-ftc-turns-up-3594404/), [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/keeping-it-real-ftc-targets-fake-reviews-in-first-consumer-review-rule)). The endorsement guides cover the founding-Pro badges | Already in line with the "no fake listings" rule; label paid placement |
| **ADA web accessibility** | **A real risk, though Persian-only lowers it.** About 5,000 digital-accessibility suits in 2025 (UsableNet); 3,117 in federal courts (+27%, Seyfarth); NY led federal filings with 1,021; California moved to state courts (Unruh Act, **$4,000 per violation**) ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/federal-court-website-accessibility-1182174/), [Equidox](https://equidox.co/?p=18626)). 64% of defendants had under $25M revenue (UsableNet, 2nd) | WCAG 2.2 AA pass before the US opens (Persian screen-reader test), an accessibility statement, and a contact route. Plaintiff firms mostly scan English sites (my inference), but Pro pages of LA businesses could attract them |
| **OFAC** | US users are US persons. GL D-2 (now 31 CFR 560.540) covers communication services ([Federal Register / OFAC](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/928096/download)), but **not trade**. The JCPOA-era permission to import Iranian carpets and foodstuffs (pistachios, caviar) was **revoked in June 2018** ([Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ofac-announces-regulations-and-guidance-implementing-withdrawal-jcpoa), [Gibson Dunn](https://gibsondunn.com/?p=32747)) | Keep the existing Iran bans (havaleh, exchange, shipping to Iran). Add a **US rule: no "new from Iran" goods** and no sourcing or import services from Iran. Ask a sanctions lawyer whether resale of Iranian-origin goods already lawfully in the US is allowed before allowing carpets and saffron in US items |
| **CAN-SPAM** | Opt-out model, sender identification, physical address | Already covered by the stricter EU double opt-in |
| **TCPA** | Covers marketing calls and texts. Verification is through the Telegram bot, so no SMS marketing. One-time codes the user requests are a different case | No marketing SMS, ever |
| **Auto-renewal laws** (e.g. California) | Consumer subscriptions | Pro is B2B, but show the renewal terms clearly anyway |

### Moderation across 9 hours

**The coverage gap:** LA is 9 hours behind Berlin. Germany 08:00–18:00 = LA 23:00–09:00, so posts made during LA's day (09:00–21:00 = 18:00–06:00 in Germany) wait 10–14 h for the owner.

**Options, combined:**
1. **Starter mode only** in the US until there is coverage. Posters are verified businesses and organisers, so the scam surface is small. Publish an SLA of «بررسی در کمتر از ۱۲ ساعت».
2. **AI pre-screening** of every post: banned terms (already listed), scam patterns, price outliers. It holds risky posts until a human looks and publishes clean posts from verified posters at once.
3. **Community flags** with automatic hiding after N flags from trusted users.
4. **A part-time Persian-speaking moderator in the Americas** (LA, Toronto or Vancouver) for 5–10 h a week in LA evenings, as a contractor through a platform (Deel or Upwork), never on an informal basis. Roughly $20–30/h (estimate). **This is the precondition for opening P2P in LA.**
5. **Follow the sun:**
   - Owner (Europe 08:00–18:00) → Americas moderator (Germany 18:00–02:00) → **Sydney is easy**: Germany 08:00–11:00 = Sydney 18:00–21:00 in the northern winter, so the owner's morning covers Sydney's evening peak.
   - A Persian-speaking moderator in Australia later closes the remaining gap.
   - The diaspora itself provides moderators in every time zone.

## 4. Canada, Australia, UK: minimum legal work for Starter (not legal advice)

### UK

- **The Online Safety Act applies.** Peydoo has user-generated listings, chat and community posts, so it is a user-to-user service. It has "links with the UK" when the UK is a target market, which opening the UK makes it ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/de-de/wissen/publications/0b658a5a/online-safety-act)). There is no small-service exemption, only lighter measures for smaller, low-risk services ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/ofcom-publishes-final-illegal-content-risk-assessment-guidance-and-codes-practice-under-uk)).
- **Minimum before opening:**
  1. A written **illegal content risk assessment** (Ofcom's 4 steps; 17 priority offence types). **Fraud** and immigration offences are the relevant ones for us. Keep records, because Ofcom runs an enforcement programme on record-keeping (since 3 Mar 2025).
  2. A **children's access assessment**. Without highly effective age assurance, the service counts as accessible to children, which brings a children's risk assessment ([Kingsley Napley](https://www.kingsleynapley.co.uk/?p=11982)). A marketplace with no adult content should come out low risk, but write it down.
  3. Duties for **reporting, complaints and terms of service**, and taking illegal content down quickly. These largely overlap with DSA Art. 16, 17 and 20, which are already built.
- **Ofcom fees:** none. Fees start at £250M qualifying worldwide revenue, with an exemption below £10M UK revenue ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/online-safety-fees-and-penalties)).
- **Enforcement is real for small foreign sites:** 4chan was fined £20k, and a suicide forum £950k (round 5).
- **UK GDPR Art. 27 representative:** needed, at roughly £150–600 a year (estimate).
- **VAT:**
  - B2C digital services from a non-UK supplier mean **registration from the first £** ([Fonoa](https://www.fonoa.com/resources/country-tax-guides/united-kingdom/tax-on-digital-services), [Quaderno](https://quaderno.io/guides/united-kingdom/vat/registration/)).
  - B2B sales are reverse-charged by the customer, and HMRC's forum confirms that treatment ([HMRC community](https://community.hmrc.gov.uk/customerforums/vat/9d38ae57-9ab8-ef11-95f6-0022481b5b6f?page=1)).
  - **So in UK Starter: sell Pro only to businesses, with evidence of business status, and no bumps to private users** until registering is worth it.
  - Ask the tax advisor what evidence of business status is enough for UK businesses below the VAT threshold.
- **DMCC Act:** the subscription regime starts **January 2027** ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2026/09/15/key-takeaways-the-subscription-shake-up-are-you-ready-for-january-2027-102o14n), [Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/subscription-reforms-brought-forward-to-january-2027-is-your-business-ready/)). It concerns consumers, so Pro (B2B) is mostly outside it. The DMCC ban on fake reviews applies anyway.
- **One-time:** risk assessments written by the owner using Ofcom's toolkit, plus a UK lawyer review, about **£1–3k** (estimate).

### Canada

- **CASL:** applies to commercial emails we send to Canadians. Express opt-in, sender identification and unsubscribe within 10 business days. Penalties up to C$10M ([getterms summary](https://getterms.io/blog/canada-anti-spam-legislation-casl), [Conestoga](https://www.conestogac.on.ca/casl/)). The double opt-in from round 5 already meets this. Keep the proof of consent.
- **PIPEDA:** no size threshold. Our GDPR setup covers it in substance; add a Canada paragraph.
- **Quebec (Bill 96 and Law 25):** businesses outside Quebec that sell to Quebec residents must serve them in French and provide commercial content in French ([Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/resources/regulations/2022/bill-96-understanding-quebec-s-french-language-law)). **Montreal (about 23k Iranians in 2016) stays out of Starter. Don't sell Pro to Quebec addresses** until there is a French version.
- **Online harms:** Bill C-63 died in January 2025 and the government plans new legislation ([canada.ca](https://www.canada.ca/en/canadian-heritage/services/online-harms.html), [Wire Report](https://www.thewirereport.ca/2025/12/22/ottawa-set-to-revive-online-harms-legislation-in-2026/)). Its 2026 status is unclear: one advocacy site claims a "Safe Social Media Act", which I did not confirm. Watch it.
- **GST/HST:** non-resident simplified registration once **sales to Canadian consumers exceed C$30k in 12 months** ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2021/07/canada-requires-non-resident-vendors-and-marketplaces-to-collect-gst-as-of-july-1.html), [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/ca/en/insights/latest-news/new-gst-hst-rules-for-digital-economy-businesses)). We will not reach that in year 1.
- **One-time:** about **C$1–2.5k** for a lawyer review (estimate).

### Australia

- **Online Safety Act 2021:**
  - **removal notices with 24 hours to comply** for designated internet services, including websites ([Gilbert + Tobin](https://gtlaw.com.au/insights/online-safety-bill-enhanced-regime));
  - **Phase 1 Standards** (from 22 Dec 2024) sort services into tiers, and the lowest tier has the fewest duties ([Allens](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2025/02/new-industry-standards-for-online-safety-what-service-providers-need-to-know/), [eSafety fact sheet](https://esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-06/Fact-sheet-Post-consultation-changes-to-the-DIS-Standard.pdf));
  - a **risk assessment** is needed for some service types;
  - our chat may count as a "relevant electronic service".
  - **Minimum:** a written self-assessment of which tier we are in, a reporting channel for Australian users, and a 24-hour takedown process (the DSA process already does this).
  - Phase 2 age-restricted-material codes since 9 Mar 2026 (round 5): a marketplace without adult categories should be low risk.
- **Privacy Act:** exempts most businesses with turnover of A$3M or less, but foreign organisations with an "Australian link" may be covered regardless, and the government plans to remove the exemption ([OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-registers/privacy-opt-in-register/opting-in-to-the-privacy-act/), [Clyde & Co](https://clydeco.com/en/insights/2023/03/small-business-exemption-consent-and-cross-border)). Treat GDPR compliance as the standard.
- **GST:** A$75k threshold for non-residents too ([Dodo Payments](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/vat-gst-registration-thresholds/)) [1 source, a vendor].
- **One-time:** about **A$1–2.5k** for a lawyer review (estimate).

## 5. Operations for one person across 10+ countries

### Moderation hours per week (estimates, at small scale)

Assumptions:
- AI pre-screening is live.
- About 1 minute per clean listing and 5–15 minutes per report or scam case.
- 15–20 minutes to verify one business.

| Unit | New posts a week | Verifications a week | Reports a week | Hours a week |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Starter country, early | 10–40 (events, services, jobs) | 3–8 businesses | 1–5 | **1.5–3** |
| Starter country, busy (e.g. US with LA, the Bay Area, DC) | 40–120 | 5–15 | 5–10 | **3–6** |
| Open city, first 3 months | 100–300 | 5–10 | 10–30 (P2P scams) | **4–8** |
| Germany base (Berlin Open plus other cities) | 300–800 | 10 | 20–50 | **8–14** |
| Guide (TR, UAE) | 0 | 0 | content upkeep | **0.5** |

**By month 12:** 5–8 Open cities plus 6–7 Starter countries plus 2 Guides comes to roughly **35–70 h a week of moderation**. **That is more than one person can do on top of seeding, sales and product work.** It needs AI pre-screening plus **two part-time moderators** (Americas and Asia-Pacific, about 10 h a week each), or the Open-city count stays at the low end. This replaces the strategy's gate "<5 h/week before the next country": that gate cannot hold in a wide plan, so it becomes **"queue age within SLA in every time zone for 6 weeks"**.

### Server

**Today:** production is a Hetzner CX33 (4 vCPU, 8 GB) in Nuremberg. Logged-out pages are cached by Cloudflare for 60 s; logged-in pages render per request (`docs/setup.md`).

**Capacity (estimate):**
- A dozen countries at early scale means maybe 3–15k visitors a day: a few requests per second on average and tens at peak. That is well within one CX33 if the 60-second cache holds.
- Meilisearch at 1 GB handles hundreds of thousands of listings.
- **Load is not the year-1 bottleneck.**

**Latency:** Nuremberg to LA is about 150 ms round trip, and to Sydney about 250–300 ms (typical figures, not measured). Cached pages are fast everywhere. For logged-in pages, Sydney adds about 0.3–0.6 s, which is acceptable for classifieds.

**What changes in the wide plan is resilience.** One server down means 12 countries down, and around Nowruz the peaks hit everywhere at once.

**Needed by month 5 (US and Australia):**
1. A tested restore from the Storage Box (a drill);
2. a documented "rebuild in under 4 h" path;
3. budget for the next server size or a warm standby (about €10–30 a month extra; check current Hetzner prices);
4. load-test one Nowruz-day scenario.

A US region is not needed in year 1.

**Staging (4 GB)** stays as it is. It must not run load tests that matter.

### Gates

**Starter (country):** no demand gate. Requirements:
1. That country's legal checklist done (§4);
2. ≥5 verified businesses or organisers lined up for day 1;
3. one local partner channel or organisation;
4. for non-EU countries, AI pre-screening live.

**City page promoted in Starter:** ≥10 verified businesses and ≥4 upcoming events.

**Open (city):** round 5's waitlist gate, held for 7 days, scaled to the city:
- Small (Vienna): 250 confirmed people / 12 businesses / 60 prepared listings.
- Medium (Randstad, Milan, Sydney): 500 / 20 / 100.
- Big (Toronto, London, LA): 1,000 / 30 / 150.

**Plus, all of these:**
1. **Moderation SLA in the city's time zone:** P2P under 12 h, for 4 weeks in Starter. LA requires the Americas moderator and Sydney the Asia-Pacific one.
2. A **local scam banner** for that market's patterns: deposit-first and wire or Zelle rental scams in the US, "holding deposit" and viewing-fee scams in the UK (my inference; write each with local sources).
3. The Berlin health check passes (next point).

**Brake on the whole plan.** If **Berlin** misses its first city gate (≥100 live listings, ≥50% of new listings get a chat within 7 days, ≥30 weekly posters) by **end of February 2027**:
- open no further Starter countries after March;
- open no Open cities outside Germany and Austria;
- spend the owner's seeding time on Berlin.

Evidence: Homejoy and Kijiji France, and round 5's "growth that outruns moderation" brake.

### Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Berlin gets diluted:** the owner's time goes to 11 other launches | **High**, the main risk | Starter needs no hand-seeding of P2P; the Berlin brake; ambassadors; one launch moment a month at most |
| Ghost-town Starter pages | Medium | Promotion only with ≥10 businesses and ≥4 events per city; the online layer fills pages; waitlist counts shown from a minimum |
| Scams outrun moderation (housing) | Medium–high where P2P is open | P2P only in Open cities with time-zone SLAs; AI hold; deposit banner; verified posters in Starter |
| Legal surprise in the UK (Ofcom) or the US (ADA suit) | Low–medium | Written OSA assessments; WCAG pass and accessibility statement; small exposure while Persian-only |
| Sanctions: US persons and Iran trade; Stripe's "citizen of Iran" clause | Medium | Existing bans plus the US goods rule; lawyer review before selling Pro in the US; SEPA or Mollie fallback (strategy) |
| One server for 12 countries; outage at Nowruz | Medium | Restore drill, headroom, cache; warm standby by month 5 |
| Tax creep (UK B2C, EU OSS) | Medium | B2B-only outside the EU; revenue tracked per country from day 1 |
| Volatility from the war: visa bans, event cancellations, user safety, regime surveillance of the diaspora (e.g. the "Alaj" platform collecting data on Iranians abroad, [Noonpost](https://www.noonpost.com/20775/?noonpdf=1)) [1 source] | High | Sober event copy; data minimisation; no political events in the Turkey or UAE Guides; quick takedown of doxxing |
| A competitor in the UK and Turkey (IraniLink app) | Medium | UK Starter in February instead of year 2 |
| Telegram verification is less common among older Iranian Americans | Unknown | Ask "Telegram?" on the waitlist sign-up; budget for SMS fallback in the US, Canada and Australia |

### Honest bottom line

- **"All countries live in one year" is achievable as Starter, Guide and the online layer**, and it is cheap (≈€9–21k one-time). It claims the brand and the SEO before others, which IraniLink's 2026 app suggests is worth doing.
- **"All countries Open in one year" is not achievable for one person.** No comparable case succeeded without either an existing network (Facebook), borrowed traffic (Kleinanzeigen) or heavy money (OLX), and the fast-expansion failures (Homejoy, Kijiji France) look exactly like that attempt.
- **Plan for 5–8 Open cities by October 2027.** Let every other city move to Open only when its gate turns green.
