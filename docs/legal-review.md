# Peydoo: legal review of the terms of use, privacy policy and Impressum

Research for the owner, to hand to a German IT/consumer-law lawyer and a tax advisor. Written 2 November 2026.

**This is not legal advice.** It is a structured review from the statutes, court decisions and official guidance listed in section F. Points marked **[uncertain]** need the lawyer's call. Texts reviewed: `src/messages/legal-de.ts` (binding German terms and privacy policy, "Stand 31. Oktober 2026"), the Persian versions in `src/messages/fa.ts` (`pages.terms`, `pages.privacy`, `impressum`, `contact`, `help`), `src/messages/operator.ts`, `src/modules/legal/versions.ts`, `seed/countries.ts`, `src/lib/region.ts`, `src/proxy.ts` (CSP) and `docs/setup.md` (legal pages, payments, Iran, countries, sign in as a user, visitor numbers).

Facts checked against the code: fonts are self-hosted (Vazirmatn, `next/font/local`), so there is no Google Fonts. The CSP allows only Cloudflare Turnstile, Stripe Checkout/Portal, Telegram OAuth (switched off) and our own image and upload hosts. Web push is planned (the `push` channel exists in the schema) but is not built yet. Cookies set by the code: `__Secure-better-auth.session_token`, `login_email`, `login_next`, `pending_favorite`, `country`, `view`, `theme`, `legal_seen`, and for staff only `view_as`, `staff_return` and `acting_blocked`. Support mail to `support@peydoo.com` is forwarded by Cloudflare Email Routing to a private **Gmail** inbox.

## A. Summary

1. **Liability (10.1):** the current clause is already the strongest form German courts accept against consumers. It can be tightened only a little (arglist, guarantee, vicarious agents, a clearer definition of "essential duties", B2B extras). The real protection has to come from outside the terms: a **UG/GmbH**, **insurance**, and a working DSA notice-and-takedown process.
2. **Wrong or outdated law:** since 19 June 2026 the service withdrawal rule is **§ 356 Abs. 5 Nr. 2 BGB**, not Abs. 4. The statutory model withdrawal notice and model form are missing. Invoice retention is now **8 years**, not 10. The Telegram controller is named wrongly (Telegram's policy names **Telegram Messenger Inc.**).
3. **Business users (P2B Regulation):** ordinary termination of a business account needs **30 days** and reasons (today 2 weeks). The terms must also set out the **ranking parameters**, including paid bumps and featured listings.
4. **Paid bumps shown without a label** in result lists may be a per-se unfair practice (**UWG Annex no. 11a**). This is the biggest Abmahnung risk. Either show a discreet "bumped" marker or get the lawyer's written approval of today's design.
5. **Account blocking:** the German Federal Court of Justice (BGH, III ZR 179/20) requires the terms to promise reasons and a chance to respond **before** a permanent block, except in urgent cases. Without that promise the blocking clauses can be void.
6. **Countries (2.1):** replace "Germany and Austria" with a rule that points to the countries shown as open on the website, plus a **country annex** for mandatory local rules. A German choice of law cannot take away a consumer's home-country protections (Rome I, Art. 6). Jurisdiction clauses work only against businesses.
7. **Privacy gaps:** support mail lands in a consumer Gmail account (no processing contract). Sanctions-list screening, automated decisions, Turnstile, the `legal_seen` cookie and the withdrawal of consent are not described. The right to object must be shown separately. The JS visitor beacon is an open question under § 25 TDDDG.
8. **Impressum:** add a second fast contact channel besides email (CJEU C-298/07). Consider a service address instead of the home address.
9. **Before opening other countries:** UK needs an Online Safety Act risk assessment, a UK GDPR representative and UK VAT from the first consumer sale. Quebec and France have French-language rules. Turkey needs VERBIS registration and a Turkish representative. US, AU and MY privacy laws most likely do not apply yet, but the US needs a DMCA agent.

## C. "On the owner's side"

### C1. The strongest clauses the law allows (ready to adapt)

**C1-1 Liability (replaces 10.1):**
> (1) Wir haften unbeschränkt bei Vorsatz und grober Fahrlässigkeit, bei der Verletzung von Leben, Körper oder Gesundheit, bei arglistigem Verschweigen eines Mangels, aus einer übernommenen Garantie, nach dem Produkthaftungsgesetz und soweit die Haftung sonst gesetzlich zwingend ist.
> (2) Verletzen wir leicht fahrlässig eine wesentliche Vertragspflicht, ist unsere Haftung auf den vertragstypischen, bei Vertragsschluss vorhersehbaren Schaden begrenzt. Wesentliche Vertragspflichten sind solche, deren Erfüllung die ordnungsgemäße Durchführung des Vertrags überhaupt erst ermöglicht und auf deren Einhaltung Sie regelmäßig vertrauen dürfen.
> (3) Im Übrigen haften wir für leichte Fahrlässigkeit nicht.
> (4) Diese Regeln gelten auch für die persönliche Haftung unserer Mitarbeiter, Vertreter und Erfüllungsgehilfen.
> (5) Nur gegenüber Unternehmern: In den Fällen von Absatz 2 ist unsere Haftung je Schadensfall auf [Betrag] und je Vertragsjahr auf [Betrag] begrenzt; Ansprüche nach Absatz 2 verjähren in einem Jahr ab dem gesetzlichen Verjährungsbeginn.

- Paragraphs (1) to (4) are the BGH-tested maximum against consumers (§ 309 Nr. 7, § 307 (2) Nr. 2 BGB).
- In (5), a cap against businesses must still cover the **typical foreseeable** loss. A cap equal to "fees paid" is risky for free business accounts. The lawyer sets the amounts (e.g. €5,000 per incident) **[uncertain]**.
- **Data loss:** "Sie sichern Inhalte, die Sie noch brauchen, selbst; bei Datenverlust haften wir nur für den Aufwand, der bei regelmäßiger Sicherung entstanden wäre." This is valid against businesses and is useful as a contributory-negligence hint against consumers.

**C1-2 Indemnity (replaces 9.2):**
> Verletzen Sie durch Ihre Inhalte oder Ihre Nutzung von Peydoo schuldhaft Rechte Dritter oder gesetzliche Vorschriften, stellen Sie uns von den daraus folgenden berechtigten Ansprüchen Dritter frei, einschließlich der angemessenen Kosten unserer Rechtsverteidigung in Höhe der gesetzlichen Gebühren. ### C3. Protection that terms cannot give (and that matters more)

1. **Legal entity.** As a sole trader the owner is personally liable with everything they own: Abmahnungen, GDPR fines and damages, copyright claims from users' photos, competitors' claims. A **UG (haftungsbeschränkt)** or **GmbH** limits this to the company's assets (except the owner's own torts and criminal acts). It also makes the business a merchant by legal form, so B2B jurisdiction clauses work. Cost is roughly €1,000–2,500 to set up, plus bookkeeping. Discuss with the tax advisor (§ 19 UStG status, and the Stripe account would move to the company).
2. **Insurance.** A professional liability policy for IT and media (*Vermögensschadenhaftpflicht* with media and IT cover, covering copyright and competition-law claims and data protection) plus cyber insurance. Several insurers sell policies for small platforms.
3. **Stay a host.** The DSA hosting exemption (Art. 6) protects as long as Peydoo does not adopt content as its own and acts quickly on notices. Voluntary moderation does not remove it (Art. 7). Risky actions: Peydoo rewriting listings, reposting them as its own posts in channels, or "verified" badges that vouch for content.
4. **US: DMCA agent.** Register a designated agent with the US Copyright Office (online, US$6, renew every 3 years) and add a repeat-infringer policy. This gives safe harbour against US copyright claims (17 U.S.C. § 512). Section 230 of the CDA already protects against most US claims about user content.
5. **Process evidence.** Store which terms version each user accepted, keep the withdrawal and consent logs (already done), and keep moderation decisions for two years (already done).
